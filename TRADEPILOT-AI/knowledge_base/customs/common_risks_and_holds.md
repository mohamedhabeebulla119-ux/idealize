# Common Causes for Customs Holds and Rejections

**From the Desk of the Risk Management Directorate, Sri Lanka Customs**

To our valued importers and trade partners, particularly Small and Medium-sized Enterprises (SMEs),

Understanding and complying with customs regulations is paramount for the efficient movement of goods across borders. The Risk Management Directorate (RMD) of Sri Lanka Customs is committed to facilitating legitimate trade while safeguarding national interests and ensuring revenue collection.

This document aims to highlight the most common reasons why shipments may experience holds or rejections, leading to significant delays, demurrage charges, and potential penalties. By understanding these issues, you can proactively implement measures to ensure smoother customs clearance.

---

### Top 5 Reasons for Customs Holds and Rejections:

#### 1. Valuation Disputes (Under-invoicing)

**Explanation:** This occurs when the declared value of imported goods is lower than their actual transaction value. Importers may intentionally under-invoice to reduce the payable customs duties and taxes. Sri Lanka Customs employs various valuation methods (e.g., transaction value, value of identical/similar goods, deductive/computed value) to determine the correct dutiable value. Discrepancies trigger investigations, requests for additional evidence, re-assessment of duties, and potential fines, leading to severe delays.

**Risk Mitigation Advice for SMEs:**
*   **Declare True Value:** Always declare the accurate and true transaction value of your goods.
*   **Maintain Records:** Keep meticulous records of purchase invoices, payment proofs (bank transfers, Letters of Credit), freight, and insurance documents readily available.
*   **Understand Incoterms:** Familiarize yourself with Incoterms (e.g., FOB, CIF) as they define the point at which costs and risks transfer, influencing the dutiable value.
*   **Seek Clarification:** If unsure about valuation rules for specific goods, consult with a licensed customs broker or approach Sri Lanka Customs for guidance *before* import.

#### 2. HS Code Misclassification

**Explanation:** The Harmonized System (HS) code is an internationally standardized system of names and numbers for classifying traded products. Assigning an incorrect HS code can lead to incorrect duty and tax rates being applied, or even bypassing import prohibitions/restrictions. Misclassification, whether accidental or intentional, requires reclassification by Customs, adjustment of duties and taxes, and can result in penalties and significant delays while the correct classification is determined.

**Risk Mitigation Advice for SMEs:**
*   **Detailed Product Information:** Provide your customs broker or agent with comprehensive details about your goods, including material composition, function, technical specifications, and intended use.
*   **Consult Resources:** Utilize the Sri Lanka Customs Tariff Guide or consult official HS classification databases.
*   **Professional Guidance:** If in doubt, engage a professional customs broker who specializes in classification or seek an advance ruling from Sri Lanka Customs for complex items.
*   **Review Regularly:** Periodically review the HS codes for your common imports, especially for new products or if product specifications change.

#### 3. Missing Mandatory Permits, Licenses, or Certificates

**Explanation:** Many goods are subject to control by Other Government Agencies (OGAs) in addition to Customs. These goods require specific import permits, licenses, or certificates (e.g., from the Telecommunications Regulatory Commission (TRC) for electronics, Sri Lanka Standards Institution (SLSI) for quality, National Medicines Regulatory Authority (NMRA) for pharmaceuticals, or Import Control Department for restricted items). Failure to obtain and present these mandatory documents *before* or *at the time of import* will result in the detention of goods, potential confiscation, and severe penalties.

**Risk Mitigation Advice for SMEs:**
*   **Pre-Import Research:** Thoroughly research all required permits, licenses, and certifications for your specific goods *before* placing an order or arranging shipment.
*   **Contact OGAs:** Directly contact the relevant Other Government Agencies to understand their requirements and application procedures.
*   **Factor in Lead Times:** Account for the time required to obtain these permits in your shipping schedule.
*   **Maintain Validity:** Ensure that all permits are valid at the time of import and retain copies for your records.

#### 4. Incomplete or Inaccurate Documentation

**Explanation:** Customs relies on accurate and complete documentation to verify shipment details, calculate duties, and process clearance. Missing documents (e.g., commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading/airway bill, certificate of origin, insurance certificate) or inconsistencies across documents (e.g., differing quantities, values, or consignee details between the invoice and packing list) will halt the clearance process. This necessitates requests for further information, corrections, and resubmission, causing avoidable delays.

**Risk Mitigation Advice for SMEs:**
*   **Documentation Checklist:** Create and consistently use a checklist of all required documents for each type of import.
*   **Accuracy is Key:** Ensure all information across all documents is consistent, accurate, and legible. Pay close attention to consignee details, quantities, descriptions, and values.
*   **Supplier Communication:** Clearly communicate your documentation requirements to your suppliers to ensure they provide all necessary and correct papers.
*   **Digital & Physical Copies:** Maintain organized digital and physical copies of all shipment documents.

#### 5. Physical Inspection Triggers

**Explanation:** While not always indicative of non-compliance, a physical inspection by Customs will inherently delay clearance. Shipments may be selected for inspection based on various risk indicators: previous non-compliance history of the importer, suspicion based on intelligence, high-risk commodities, country of origin, random selection protocols, or discrepancies identified during document examination. While necessary for Customs control and revenue assurance, inspections add to the clearance time.

**Risk Mitigation Advice for SMEs:**
*   **Strong Compliance Record:** Consistently maintain a clean compliance record through accurate declarations, timely payment of duties, and adherence to regulations. This helps establish a low-risk profile.
*   **Goods Match Declaration:** Ensure that the actual goods in the container precisely match the descriptions, quantities, and other details provided in your declarations.
*   **Reputable Partners:** Work with reliable suppliers and experienced, licensed customs brokers or agents.
*   **Facilitate Inspection:** If selected for inspection, ensure goods are packed and labeled appropriately to facilitate easy access and examination by Customs officers. Respond promptly to any requests.
