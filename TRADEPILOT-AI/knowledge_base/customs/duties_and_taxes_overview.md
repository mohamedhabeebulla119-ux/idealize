# Overview of Duties and Taxes on Imports in Sri Lanka

**Authority:** Sri Lanka Customs
**Last Updated:** March 2026

## 1. Introduction
The cost of importing goods into Sri Lanka is primarily determined by a combination of customs duties, statutory levies, and value-added taxes. These are calculated based on the CIF (Cost, Insurance, and Freight) value of the imported consignment.

## 2. Primary Duties and Levies

### 2.1 Customs Import Duty (CID)
The standard tariff applied to imported goods.
*   **Rates:** Typically falls into bands of 0%, 10%, or 15% depending on the essential nature of the item and local industry protection policies.
*   **Calculation:** Applied directly to the CIF value.

### 2.2 Value Added Tax (VAT)
A consumption tax applied to the value added to goods.
*   **Current Rate:** 18% (Standard Rate).
*   **Calculation Base:** VAT is calculated on the aggregate of the CIF value plus all other applicable duties and levies (i.e., CIF + CID + PAL + CESS + Excise Duty).

### 2.3 Ports and Airports Development Levy (PAL)
A levy collected to fund infrastructure development.
*   **Rates:** Generally 10%, with a concessionary rate of 5% for specific machinery and raw materials.
*   **Calculation:** Applied directly to the CIF value.

### 2.4 CESS (Commodity Export Subsidy Scheme)
A levy primarily intended to protect local industries or fund specific sectors.
*   **Rates:** Highly variable. Can be a specific rate (e.g., Rs. X per kg) or an ad valorem rate (percentage of the value).

## 3. Example Calculation
For an item with CIF value of Rs. 100,000, assuming 15% CID, 10% PAL, and 18% VAT (ignoring CESS for simplicity):
*   **CID:** Rs. 15,000
*   **PAL:** Rs. 10,000
*   **Base for VAT:** Rs. 100,000 + 15,000 + 10,000 = Rs. 125,000
*   **VAT (18%):** Rs. 22,500
*   **Total Taxes:** Rs. 47,500
