# SRI LANKA CUSTOMS - 2026 TARIFF SCHEDULE SUMMARY

**Colombo, Sri Lanka – January 1st, 2026**

Sri Lanka Customs is pleased to present a summary of the 2026 Tariff Schedule, reflecting the government's ongoing commitment to fostering economic growth, promoting local industries, ensuring public welfare, and optimizing revenue collection. The 2026 schedule continues to build upon principles of rationalization and predictability, while strategically adjusting duties to align with national development priorities.

## Key Policy Highlights for 2026:

*   **Strategic Import Substitution & Export Promotion:** Continued emphasis on lower duties for critical raw materials and intermediate goods required by export-oriented and value-added manufacturing sectors.
*   **Support for Essential Services:** Sustained low or zero-duty regimes for vital necessities such as essential medical supplies and certain food items.
*   **Revenue Optimization:** Maintain higher duty rates on non-essential and luxury goods to manage foreign exchange outflows and maximize revenue.
*   **Green Customs Initiatives:** Introduction of preferential tariffs for environmentally friendly technologies and goods, alongside potentially higher duties on products with significant negative environmental impacts.
*   **Digitalization and Simplification:** Ongoing efforts to streamline customs procedures and enhance efficiency through digital platforms, though not directly a tariff policy.

## Standard Customs Import Duty (CID) and Port and Airport Levy (PAL) Bands:

The following duty bands are indicative and apply to most items within these categories, unless specific HS codes dictate otherwise due to international agreements, special concessions, or specific policy directives.

### 1. Essential Medical Supplies

*   **Customs Import Duty (CID):** 0%
*   **Port and Airport Levy (PAL):** 0%
    *   *Rationale:* To ensure accessibility, affordability, and public health, essential pharmaceuticals, medical equipment, and critical supplies remain largely exempt from CID and PAL.

### 2. Raw Materials for Manufacturing

*   **Customs Import Duty (CID):** 0% - 5%
*   **Port and Airport Levy (PAL):** 0% - 2.5%
    *   *Rationale:* To support local manufacturing, enhance competitiveness, and promote value addition, raw materials critical for industries (especially those contributing to exports or import substitution) benefit from minimal duty rates. Higher rates within this band apply to raw materials where a nascent local supply exists.

### 3. Consumer Electronics

*   **Customs Import Duty (CID):** 15% - 25%
*   **Port and Airport Levy (PAL):** 7.5% - 10%
    *   *Rationale:* While considered semi-essential by some, consumer electronics generally attract moderate duties to generate revenue and manage non-essential imports. Rates vary based on the specific type of electronic device, with higher-end items attracting duties at the upper end of the band.

### 4. Luxury Vehicles

*   **Customs Import Duty (CID):** 100% - 150%
*   **Port and Airport Levy (PAL):** 10% - 15%
    *   *Rationale:* Luxury vehicles continue to attract significantly high duties. This policy aims to conserve foreign exchange, discourage conspicuous consumption, and maximize revenue from high-value imports. It's important to note that luxury vehicles also incur substantial Excise Duty, VAT, and other levies, which further increase their landed cost.

## How Total Tax is Compounded:

The total import tax liability in Sri Lanka is a multi-layered calculation where certain duties and levies are calculated on a base that already includes previous taxes. This creates a compounding effect.

The general compounding sequence is as follows:

1.  **CIF Value:** This is the base value of the imported goods (Cost + Insurance + Freight) at the port of entry.

2.  **Customs Import Duty (CID):**
    *   Calculated as a percentage of the CIF Value.
    *   `CID Amount = CIF Value × CID Rate`

3.  **Port and Airport Levy (PAL):**
    *   Calculated as a percentage of the **(CIF Value + CID Amount)**.
    *   `PAL Amount = (CIF Value + CID Amount) × PAL Rate`

4.  **Excise Duty (if applicable):**
    *   For goods like vehicles, tobacco, alcohol, etc., Excise Duty is a significant component.
    *   It is typically calculated on a base that includes the **(CIF Value + CID Amount + PAL Amount)**, and sometimes other charges or a prescribed specific value.

5.  **Value Added Tax (VAT):**
    *   VAT is calculated on the **"Taxable Value"** of the import.
    *   The "Taxable Value" generally includes the **(CIF Value + CID Amount + PAL Amount + Excise Duty Amount)** and any other specific levies (e.g., Cess).
    *   `VAT Amount = Taxable Value × VAT Rate`

6.  **Nation Building Tax (NBT) / Social Security Contribution Levy (SSCL) and other Levies (if applicable):**
    *   Depending on the specific item and prevailing tax laws, other levies such as NBT (if still applicable, or its successor like SSCL which applies from October 2022 and continues into 2026) may be applied.
    *   These are usually calculated on a similar taxable base as VAT, or specifically on the CIF value, or on the aggregate of previous duties.

**In essence, the CID increases the base for PAL, and the sum of CIF + CID + PAL (and Excise, if applicable) forms the base for VAT and other subsequent levies, leading to a compounded total tax burden.**
