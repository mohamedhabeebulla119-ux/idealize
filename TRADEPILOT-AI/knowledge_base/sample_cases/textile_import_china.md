# GUIDELINE FOR IMPORTATION OF WOVEN FABRICS OF COTTON (HS CODE 5208) FROM CHINA

**Issued by: Sri Lanka Customs**
**Date: October 26, 2023**

---

This guideline provides essential information for commercial importers planning to import Woven Fabrics of Cotton (HS Code 5208) from the People's Republic of China into Sri Lanka. It outlines the methodology for calculating applicable tariffs and taxes, as well as the necessary documentation for customs clearance.

## 1. Introduction

Sri Lanka Customs is committed to facilitating legitimate trade while ensuring compliance with all national laws and regulations. This document aims to assist importers in understanding their obligations and streamlining the import process for woven cotton fabrics under HS Code 5208.

## 2. Harmonized System (HS) Classification

*   **HS Code:** 5208 - Woven fabrics of cotton, containing 85% or more by weight of cotton, weighing not more than 200 g/m².
*   **Importance:** It is crucial for importers to accurately classify their specific product using the full 8-digit or 10-digit HS Code (e.g., 5208.11.00 for plain weave, unbleached, etc.). Incorrect classification can lead to penalties, delays, and incorrect duty assessments. Importers are encouraged to verify the specific subheading with Customs or a licensed customs broker.

## 3. Applicable Tariffs and Taxes Calculation

The total amount payable to Sri Lanka Customs for imported goods typically includes a combination of Customs Import Duty (CID), Value Added Tax (VAT), Port & Airport Levy (PAL), and potentially a Cess and Social Security Contribution Levy (SSCL). These are generally calculated on the **Customs Value (CIF - Cost, Insurance, Freight) in Sri Lankan Rupees (LKR)**.

### a. Key Components of Customs Duties & Taxes:

1.  **Customs Import Duty (CID):** An *ad valorem* duty (a percentage of the CIF value). The rate for textiles can vary, often falling into categories like 0%, 5%, 15%, or 30%. For HS 5208, it is **imperative to check the current gazetted rate for the specific 8-digit HS subheading.** *For the purpose of this guideline, we will use an illustrative rate of 15%*.
2.  **Port & Airport Levy (PAL):** An *ad valorem* levy, typically **5%** of the CIF value.
3.  **Cess Levy:** A special levy imposed on certain goods to protect local industries or generate revenue. Cess rates can be *ad valorem* (percentage of CIF), specific (per unit, e.g., per kg or per meter), or a combination. **It is essential to verify if Cess is applicable to your specific HS subheading of 5208 and the prevailing rate.** *For the purpose of this guideline, we will use an illustrative rate of 10% (ad valorem) assuming it is applicable.*
4.  **Social Security Contribution Levy (SSCL):** A levy introduced recently, currently at **2.5%**. For imports, it is calculated on the aggregate of the CIF value and any applicable Customs Import Duty, PAL, and Cess.
5.  **Value Added Tax (VAT):** The current standard rate is **18%**. VAT is calculated on the aggregate of the CIF value, CID, PAL, and Cess.

### b. Step-by-Step Calculation Methodology:

Let's assume the following for an illustrative calculation:
*   **CIF Value (USD):** USD 10,000
*   **Exchange Rate:** LKR 300 / USD
*   **CID Rate:** 15%
*   **PAL Rate:** 5%
*   **Cess Rate:** 10% (if applicable)
*   **SSCL Rate:** 2.5%
*   **VAT Rate:** 18%

**Step 1: Convert CIF Value to LKR**
*   LKR CIF Value = USD 10,000 * LKR 300/USD = **LKR 3,000,000**

**Step 2: Calculate Customs Import Duty (CID)**
*   CID = LKR CIF Value * CID Rate
*   CID = LKR 3,000,000 * 0.15 = **LKR 450,000**

**Step 3: Calculate Port & Airport Levy (PAL)**
*   PAL = LKR CIF Value * PAL Rate
*   PAL = LKR 3,000,000 * 0.05 = **LKR 150,000**

**Step 4: Calculate Cess Levy (if applicable)**
*   Cess = LKR CIF Value * Cess Rate (if ad valorem)
*   Cess = LKR 3,000,000 * 0.10 = **LKR 300,000**

**Step 5: Calculate Social Security Contribution Levy (SSCL)**
*   SSCL Base = LKR CIF Value + CID + PAL + Cess
*   SSCL Base = LKR 3,000,000 + LKR 450,000 + LKR 150,000 + LKR 300,000 = LKR 3,900,000
*   SSCL = SSCL Base * SSCL Rate
*   SSCL = LKR 3,900,000 * 0.025 = **LKR 97,500**

**Step 6: Calculate Value Added Tax (VAT)**
*   VAT Base = LKR CIF Value + CID + PAL + Cess
*   VAT Base = LKR 3,000,000 + LKR 450,000 + LKR 150,000 + LKR 300,000 = LKR 3,900,000
*   VAT = VAT Base * VAT Rate
*   VAT = LKR 3,900,000 * 0.18 = **LKR 702,000**

**Step 7: Calculate Total Payable to Customs**
*   Total Payable = CID + PAL + Cess + SSCL + VAT
*   Total Payable = LKR 450,000 + LKR 150,000 + LKR 300,000 + LKR 97,500 + LKR 702,000 = **LKR 1,699,500**

---
**Disclaimer on Rates:** All duty and tax rates (CID, PAL, Cess, SSCL, VAT) and exchange rates are subject to change by gazette notifications issued by the Ministry of Finance and other relevant authorities. Importers must refer to the latest applicable gazettes and the Sri Lanka Customs website for the most current rates at the time of importation. The example provided is purely illustrative.

## 4. Documentation Required for Customs Clearance

To ensure a smooth and efficient customs clearance process for woven cotton fabrics from China, the following documents are typically required:

### a. Mandatory Documents:

1.  **Commercial Invoice:**
    *   Original invoice from the seller.
    *   Must clearly state the full name and address of the seller and buyer, date, invoice number, detailed description of goods (including quantity, unit price, total value, HS Code), currency, and Incoterm (e.g., FOB, CIF).
2.  **Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB):**
    *   Original or Express Release copy.
    *   Issued by the shipping line or airline, confirming receipt of goods and terms of carriage.
3.  **Packing List:**
    *   Detailed list showing contents, weight (gross and net), dimensions, and marking of each package.
4.  **Certificate of Origin (CoO):**
    *   Issued by the competent authority in China (e.g., China Council for the Promotion of International Trade - CCPIT).
    *   Confirms the origin of the goods. While China does not have a comprehensive Free Trade Agreement with Sri Lanka offering significant duty concessions on general goods, a CoO is still important for origin verification.
5.  **Customs Declaration (CUSDEC):**
    *   Submitted electronically through the ASYCUDA World system by the importer or their licensed customs broker.
    *   Contains all relevant details of the shipment for duty and tax assessment.
6.  **Importer's Business Registration (BR) Certificate:**
    *   Proof of valid business registration in Sri Lanka.
7.  **Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN):**
    *   Registered with the Department of Inland Revenue.
8.  **Bank Documents:**
    *   Letter of Credit (L/C) copies, Telegraphic Transfer (T/T) copies, or other proof of payment/transaction as required by banks and regulatory bodies (e.g., Central Bank of Sri Lanka).
9.  **Insurance Certificate/Policy:**
    *   If the goods are imported under CIF terms, indicating the insurance coverage.

### b. Potentially Required / Additional Documents:

1.  **Import Control License (ICL):**
    *   While general woven cotton fabrics (HS 5208) are typically not subject to Import Control License requirements, it is prudent to verify the latest Import Control Regulations (ICR) issued by the Controller General of Imports and Exports. Certain specific types of fabrics or textiles for particular end-uses *might* require an ICL.
2.  **Quality Certificates / Test Reports:**
    *   Depending on the specific application or if mandated by Sri Lankan standards bodies (e.g., Sri Lanka Standards Institution - SLSI), certificates related to fabric composition, weave, dye fastness, strength, or other quality parameters may be requested.
3.  **Purchase Order / Sales Contract:**
    *   Supporting document showing the agreed terms of trade between the buyer and seller.
4.  **Freight Invoice:**
    *   If the freight cost is not explicitly itemized on the commercial invoice (e.g., for FOB shipments where freight is arranged separately).

## 5. Customs Clearance Process Overview

1.  **Submission of CUSDEC:** The importer or their customs broker electronically submits the CUSDEC via the ASYCUDA World system, along with scanned copies of supporting documents.
2.  **Assessment:** Customs officers review the CUSDEC and supporting documents to verify classification, valuation, and calculate applicable duties and taxes.
3.  **Payment:** Upon assessment, the importer pays the determined duties, taxes, and levies to Sri Lanka Customs through designated banks or online payment gateways.
4.  **Examination (if selected):** Shipments may be selected for physical examination based on risk assessment criteria.
5.  **Release:** Once all duties and taxes are paid and clearance procedures are completed, Customs issues the release order for the goods.

## 6. Important Notes & Recommendations

*   **Accuracy:** Always ensure that all information provided in your declarations and supporting documents is accurate, truthful, and consistent. Misdeclarations can lead to penalties, seizure of goods, or legal action.
*   **Stay Updated:** Customs duties, taxes, and import regulations are subject to frequent changes. Importers are responsible for keeping abreast of the latest gazettes, circulars, and notifications.
*   **Engage a Licensed Customs Broker:** It is highly recommended to engage the services of a licensed customs broker in Sri Lanka. They possess the expertise and knowledge to navigate complex customs procedures, ensure accurate declarations, and expedite the clearance process.
*   **Record Keeping:** Maintain comprehensive records of all import transactions for a minimum period of 5 years, as required by law.
*   **Valuation:** Sri Lanka Customs adheres to the WTO Valuation Agreement. All declared values must be accurate and verifiable.
