# Formal Compliance Roadmap for Exporting Ceylon Tea (HS Code 0902) to the United Kingdom

**Issued by:** Sri Lanka Tea Board
**Date:** October 26, 2023
**Reference:** SLTB/EX/UK/2023-01

---

## 1. Introduction and Purpose

This document outlines the mandatory compliance roadmap for Sri Lankan tea exporters engaged in the trade of Ceylon Tea (HS Code 0902) to the United Kingdom. As the regulatory authority for the Sri Lankan tea industry, the Sri Lanka Tea Board (SLTB) is committed to facilitating smooth, compliant, and sustainable export operations while upholding the integrity and quality standards of Ceylon Tea.

This roadmap covers essential registrations, specific Tea Board permits, UK import requirements including phytosanitary/food safety standards, customs duties, and an estimated timeline for the entire process.

---

## 2. Mandatory Registrations in Sri Lanka

Before commencing any export activities, prospective exporters must complete the following foundational registrations:

### 2.1 General Business Registrations

*   **Registration of Company:** Companies must be formally registered with the Department of Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act No. 07 of 2007.
*   **Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN):** Obtain a TIN from the Inland Revenue Department (IRD).
*   **Bank Account:** A dedicated corporate bank account with a commercial bank in Sri Lanka is essential for financial transactions.
*   **Export Development Board (EDB) Registration (Recommended):** While not strictly mandatory for all tea exports, registration with the EDB is highly recommended to access promotional activities, market information, and potential incentives.

### 2.2 Sri Lanka Tea Board (SLTB) Specific Registrations

All entities involved in the production, blending, packing, or export of tea must register with the Sri Lanka Tea Board.

*   **Tea Exporter Registration:**
    *   **Requirement:** Mandatory for all companies wishing to export tea.
    *   **Process:** Submit an application to the SLTB Export Division with required documentation (company registration, director details, business plan, etc.).
    *   **Validity:** Renewable annually.
    *   **Compliance:** Adherence to the Tea Board Act No. 15 of 1975 and subsequent regulations is paramount.
*   **Tea Factory Registration (for producers):** If the exporter also owns a tea factory, the factory must be registered and licensed by the SLTB, ensuring adherence to quality and hygiene standards.
*   **Tea Packer/Blender Registration (for value-added exporters):** Facilities involved in blending and packaging tea must be registered and periodically inspected by the SLTB.
*   **"Ceylon Tea" Lion Logo Usage Agreement:** Exporters wishing to use the iconic "Lion Logo" for Ceylon Tea must enter into a licensing agreement with the SLTB and comply with its usage guidelines, which guarantee 100% Sri Lankan origin tea.

## 3. Tea Board Export Permit and Certification

### 3.1 Export Permit Application

*   **Requirement:** Every consignment of tea leaving Sri Lanka requires an export permit from the SLTB.
*   **Application Process:**
    *   Submit a duly filled export permit application form to the SLTB.
    *   Provide supporting documents:
        *   Commercial Invoice
        *   Packing List
        *   Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (draft acceptable initially)
        *   Customs Declaration Form (CUSDEC)
        *   Proof of payment (Letter of Credit, Advance Payment confirmation, etc.)
        *   Quality certificates (e.g., MRL test reports, HACCP, ISO 22000, organic certification if applicable).
    *   **Pre-Shipment Inspection:** The SLTB may conduct a pre-shipment inspection to verify the quality, quantity, and packaging of the tea against the declared standards and international requirements.
*   **Issuance:** Upon satisfaction of all requirements, the SLTB will issue the export permit.

### 3.2 Certificate of Origin

*   **Requirement:** While a general Certificate of Origin can be obtained from the Department of Commerce, the SLTB also issues a Certificate of Origin specifically attesting to the "Ceylon Tea" designation. This is crucial for verifying the authenticity and origin of the product.
*   **Benefit:** May be required by UK customs or the importer for preferential trade agreements, though the UK Global Tariff for tea is often 0%.

### 3.3 Quality Assurance and Standards

*   **Sri Lanka Standards Institution (SLSI):** Adherence to relevant SLSI standards for tea is mandatory.
*   **International Certifications (Recommended):** While not always mandatory for market entry, certifications such as ISO 22000 (Food Safety Management), HACCP, GMP, Fairtrade, Rainforest Alliance, and Organic (USDA, EU, COR for UK organic) are highly valued in the UK market and significantly enhance market access and consumer confidence. The SLTB encourages exporters to obtain these.

## 4. UK Import Requirements

Exporters must ensure their consignment complies with all relevant UK regulations for food imports.

### 4.1 Phytosanitary and Food Safety Requirements

For processed tea (HS 0902), traditional phytosanitary certificates (as for fresh produce) are generally *not* required. However, stringent food safety standards are paramount.

*   **Pesticide Residue Limits (MRLs):** Compliance with UK-retained EU Maximum Residue Limits (MRLs) for pesticides is critical. Exporters must ensure their tea is tested and meets these strict limits. Non-compliance can lead to rejection and destruction of the consignment.
*   **Contaminants:** Tea must be free from harmful levels of contaminants such as heavy metals (e.g., lead, cadmium), mycotoxins (e.g., Ochratoxin A), and foreign matter.
*   **Food Information Regulations:** Packaging and labelling must comply with UK food information to consumers regulations, including ingredient lists, allergen declarations, nutritional information, origin, and best-before dates.
*   **Organic Certification (if applicable):** If the tea is marketed as organic, it must be certified by a UK-approved organic control body (e.g., Soil Association, Organic Farmers & Growers).
*   **Importer's Responsibility:** The UK importer is legally responsible for ensuring the safety and compliance of the food product. It is crucial for exporters to work closely with their importer to meet specific documentation and inspection requirements by agencies like the Food Standards Agency (FSA) and the Department for Environment, Food & Rural Affairs (Defra).
*   **TRACE-NT System:** While not typically required for processed tea, importers must be aware of potential changes or specific requirements for certain food products that may necessitate pre-notification via the UK's TRACES-NT system. Exporters should confirm this with their UK importer.

### 4.2 Customs Duties and Taxes (UK Global Tariff - UKGT)

*   **HS Code 0902:** This covers "Tea, whether or not flavoured."
*   **Standard Customs Duty:** Under the current UK Global Tariff (UKGT), most forms of processed tea (HS 0902) imported into the UK face a **0% customs duty**.
    *   *Note: This is subject to change. Exporters should always verify the latest tariff information on the UK government's trade tariff website prior to shipment.*
*   **Value Added Tax (VAT):**
    *   Generally, most food products in the UK are zero-rated for VAT. However, prepared or packaged beverages (like tea in teabags, instant tea) are typically subject to the standard VAT rate (currently 20%). Loose leaf tea sold for preparation by the consumer may be zero-rated.
    *   Exporters should confirm the specific VAT treatment with their UK importer, as this depends on the product's final form and packaging.
*   **Rules of Origin:** While the 0% duty reduces the urgency for preferential origin for tea, it's good practice to ensure proper documentation of origin in case of future tariff changes or specific trade agreement requirements.

### 4.3 UK Importer Responsibilities

The UK importer plays a crucial role in the clearance process:

*   **EORI Number:** The UK importer must have an EORI (Economic Operator Registration and Identification) number.
*   **Customs Declaration:** The importer (or their customs agent) must submit an import customs declaration via the UK's Customs Declaration Service (CDS).
*   **Port Health Authority Checks:** Consignments may be subject to documentary, identity, and physical checks by UK Port Health Authorities or other designated bodies.

## 5. Estimated Timeline

The following is an estimated timeline for the initial setup and export of Ceylon Tea to the UK. Actual durations may vary significantly based on individual company efficiency, responsiveness of regulatory bodies, and specific product requirements.

*   **Phase 1: Company & General Business Registration (Sri Lanka)**
    *   Duration: 2-4 weeks
    *   Activities: Company registration, TIN, bank account setup.
*   **Phase 2: Sri Lanka Tea Board Registration**
    *   Duration: 2-3 weeks (after Phase 1)
    *   Activities: Exporter registration, factory/packer registration, Lion Logo agreement.
*   **Phase 3: Quality Certifications & Product Preparation**
    *   Duration: 4-12 weeks (can run concurrently with Phase 1 & 2)
    *   Activities: SLSI compliance, obtaining international certifications (ISO, HACCP, Organic), MRL testing, product formulation, packaging design.
*   **Phase 4: Order Confirmation & Export Permit Application**
    *   Duration: 1-2 weeks (per consignment)
    *   Activities: Finalizing order with UK importer, preparing shipping documents, SLTB export permit application, pre-shipment inspection.
*   **Phase 5: Shipping & UK Customs Clearance**
    *   Duration: 2-6 weeks (depending on mode of transport – sea vs. air, and customs efficiency)
    *   Activities: Ocean/air freight booking, loading, transit, UK customs clearance, final delivery.

**Total Estimated Lead Time for First Shipment:** 12 - 27 weeks (approximately 3 to 7 months). Subsequent shipments will be significantly faster (Phase 4 & 5 only).

## 6. Ongoing Compliance and Best Practices

*   **Stay Updated:** Regularly monitor changes in UK import regulations, food safety standards, and customs tariffs. The SLTB will endeavor to disseminate relevant updates.
*   **Traceability:** Implement robust traceability systems from "tea bush to cup" to ensure quick identification and recall if necessary.
*   **Continuous Improvement:** Strive for continuous improvement in quality, food safety, and sustainable practices.
*   **Market Intelligence:** Understand UK consumer preferences, market trends, and competitive landscape.
*   **Documentation:** Maintain meticulous records of all transactions, certifications, and compliance documents.

---

## 7. Disclaimer

This roadmap provides general guidance based on current regulations. It is not exhaustive and should not be considered legal advice. Exporters are strongly advised to:

*   Consult directly with the Sri Lanka Tea Board for the most up-to-date local requirements.
*   Engage with a reputable UK importer or customs broker for advice on UK-specific import regulations, duties, and taxes.
*   Verify all information with official government sources in both Sri Lanka and the United Kingdom before making export decisions.
