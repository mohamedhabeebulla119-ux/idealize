# General Import and Export Licensing Rules for SMEs

**Authority:** Department of Import and Export Control
**Date:** January 15, 2026

## 1. Introduction
This guide outlines the standard operating procedures for Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) in Sri Lanka to obtain the necessary licensing for import and export activities.

## 2. General Requirements
Before engaging in cross-border trade, an SME must be formally registered and recognized by the relevant trade authorities.
*   **Business Registration (BR):** Must be registered under the Companies Act or as a sole proprietorship/partnership.
*   **Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN):** Mandatory for all import/export activities, issued by the Department of Inland Revenue.
*   **Bank References:** A letter of introduction/reference from a commercial bank operating in Sri Lanka.

## 3. Obtaining an Import/Export License
Certain goods are subject to import or export control under the Import and Export (Control) Act No. 1 of 1969.
1.  **Check the Gazette:** Verify if your product's HS Code falls under the restricted or controlled list published in the latest government gazette.
2.  **Application Submission:** Submit the completed application form along with the Proforma Invoice to the Department of Import and Export Control.
3.  **Processing Timeline:** Standard applications are processed within 3 to 5 working days, provided all documentation is complete and accurate.

## 4. Exemptions
Goods imported for personal use, samples of no commercial value, and specific items approved by the Board of Investment (BOI) may be exempt from standard commercial licensing requirements.
